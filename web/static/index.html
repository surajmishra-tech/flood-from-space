            Images used

            What this system cannot do (honest limits)
            • No early warning. Sentinel-1 passes a valley only every few days, so it maps damage after the event - it cannot warn minutes before a glacier collapse.
            • 10 m pixels. Small houses, narrow roads and footbridges can be missed. Official EMS maps use 0.3–0.7 m images.
            • Steep gorges. Radar can't see some steep slopes (layover / shadow), and the 30 m height model is rough in narrow valleys, so we under-map the edges of the debris.
            • Only the valley floor is checked (low above the river). Landslides high on slopes are not mapped.
            • "Hit" is not "destroyed". We say a building or road touches the flood area; we don't grade the damage.
            • OpenStreetMap gaps. Missing buildings, roads or villages mean missing answers. Footpaths, trails and footbridges are not in the road network.
            • Roads outside the box are assumed to be open.
            • Help requests are a demo. They are saved on this computer and shown on this screen only; nothing reaches a real government service. They are protected by one shared PIN, not by real user accounts. Requests are accepted only from phones inside a mapped flood area: this stops casual fakes, but GPS can be faked, and a real person outside our maps is shown the emergency numbers instead.
            • Educational prototype - not an operational tool.

            The evidence

            How we know the map is right

            Every answer on the dashboard comes from free satellite data and the map as it was before the flood. Here is the proof, step by step, for the Trishuli flood of 26 August 2026.

            RadarPhotosHow it worksOfficial check No-flood testOther floodsAIPriorityLimits
            01

            Radar sees the flood through the clouds

            Sentinel-1 sends its own radar waves, so it works through cloud, day and night. We compare the same valley before and after the flood. Drag the line to compare. This is the 3.8 km around Rasuwa Gadhi, where the radar changed most.

            02

            Why radar: the photo after the flood is full of clouds

            Sentinel-2 takes normal colour photos, but monsoon clouds hide the valley. Only 67.9% of the valley was cloud-free after the flood, so photos only confirm the radar where the sky is clear.

            03

            How it works, from satellite to answer

            Draw a box, give the flood date, press Run. Everything below happens by itself in 2–6 minutes, for any area and any date.

            1. Sentinel-1 radar3 passes before and 2 after, from both directions; change detection finds new mud, debris and water.
            2. Copernicus height modelOnly the valley floor is checked (low above the river, not too steep), so mountain slopes don't give false alarms.
            3. Sentinel-2 photosWhere the sky is clear, the photos confirm the radar (water and bare mud look different from forest).
            4. Possible damageA second, lighter map: land next to a real damage patch that is no higher above the river than the debris we confirmed there. The confirmed map stays separate.
            5. Map from before the floodOpenStreetMap as it was before the flood: buildings, roads, bridges, villages, hospitals.
            6. Road networkBroken roads are removed; a village with no road left to a town or hospital is cut off, then ranked by rescue priority.
            7. AI copilotWrites the situation report in English or Nepali. It writes only blanks; our code fills in every number.
            04

            Checked against the official EU emergency map

            Copernicus EMS mapped this flood by hand from very sharp images (0.3–0.7 m). We used them only to check our result, never as an input.

            Honest reading: what we map as confirmed is almost always real (high precision), but alone it misses part of the outer edge of the debris, where radar and photos show little change. The lighter possible damage zone (land next to real damage that is no higher above the river) closes most of that gap, and it adds nothing on the no-flood test below. Its four settings were picked after checking against these maps, so we show both numbers.

            05

            No flood, no alarm

            The same box on a dry-season date (15 April 2026), run with the same settings. A system that "sees" floods everywhere would fail this test.

            06

            Not built for one valley: the same system on other floods

            No settings were changed between these runs. Each one is a saved result you can open.

            07

            An AI that cannot invent numbers

            In a disaster, a wrong number can send a rescue team to the wrong place. So the language model is only allowed to write blanks; a checker rejects any digit it writes, and our code fills each blank from the maps.

            09

            For people in the flood, and what we cannot do

            "I need help" side

            People inside a mapped flood area can check their danger level, call emergency numbers, and send their GPS location to the rescue team's inbox (PIN-protected). Requests from outside the flood area, or far from the chosen place, are refused, which stops casual fake requests.

            Open the "I need help" side →

            Honest limits

            • No early warning: satellites pass every few days, so we map damage after the event.
            • 10 m pixels: small houses, narrow roads and footbridges can be missed.
            • Steep gorges: radar can't see some slopes, so we under-map debris edges.
            • "Hit" is not "destroyed": we don't grade damage.
            • Map gaps: missing buildings or roads in OpenStreetMap mean missing answers.
            • An educational prototype, not an operational tool.
